<h1>OSINT Exercise #031 — Giraffe Conservation Investigation - Gralhix - by Sofia Santos</h1>
<a href="https://gralhix.com/list-of-osint-exercises/osint-exercise-031/">Source</a>

<h2>Case</h2>
<p>The newborn giraffe seen in the photo below belongs to an endangered species. Individuals such as this one, born in captivity, often participate in conservation programs. This may include transfers to accredited facilities for carefully planned breeding efforts aimed at ensuring the survival of their species.</p>

<h2>Objective</h2>
<p>Find the following information regarding the individual seen in the photo:</p>
<ul style="list-style-type: '- '; padding-left: 20px;">
  <li>Location and date of birth.</li>
  <li>Current residence and date of arrival.</li>
  <li>A photo in its current habitat.</li>
</ul>

<img src="https://i0.wp.com/gralhix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/osintexercise031.png?resize=402%2C539&ssl=1"/>

<h2>Skills Practiced</h2>
<ul style="list-style-type: '- '; padding-left: 20px;">
  <li>Reverse image search and source tracing</li>
  <li>Cross-referencing multiple sources for verification</li>
  <li>Evidence preservation using the Wayback Machine</li>
  <li>Open-source research using news articles and blog posts</li>
</ul>

<h2>Tools Used</h2>
<ul style="list-style-type: '- '; padding-left: 20px;">
  <li>TinEye — Reverse image search to locate original source and metadata</li>
  <li>Google Lens — image identification and reverse image search</li>
  <li>Wayback Machine — Discovery of historical zoo records and preservation of volatile evidence</li>
  <li>Google Search & Advanced Dorking — Manual keyword research to identify subjects and cross-reference arrival dates</li>
</ul>

<h3>Step 2 — Initial Identification and Lead Generation</h3>

<img width="565" height="158" alt="Screenshot (53)" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d8eda998-fc81-4e55-99ab-6dece7490c72" />

<i>Ref 4:  Initial identification leads regarding the subject’s name and history</i>

<p>To establish a baseline for the investigation, I used image recognition to generate initial leads. This process provided several key data points to be manually verified: the subject was identified as a giraffe named Willow, born on October 21, 2009, at the Virginia Zoo in Norfolk.<br><br>

The preliminary results also suggested a significant lead for the second part of the task: a transfer to Disney’s Animal Kingdom. Additionally, I noted a reference to a distinct heart-shaped marking on her neck, which I recorded as a primary visual identifier for cross-referencing in later steps. I treated these findings not as final answers, but as a technical foundation for the targeted manual searches that followed.
</p>

<h3>Step 3 — Google Search for arrival date at DAK, WDW Magazine Website, Windsor Palms Florida News Website, LaughingPlace.com , and Flickr Post</h3>

<p>I searched: <i>"willow giraffe disney animal kingdom"</i>. In the overview I found a link to the WDW Magazine website discussing the arrival of a new baby giraffe at Disney's Animal Kingdom. I clicked on the link to get more information about Willow's arrival date. It turned out to be a different giraffe — a male, born on June 10 — not Willow.</p>

<p>After scrolling through hyperlinks, I found a news article from the Windsor Palms, Florida news webpage titled: <i>"Disney's Animal Kingdom Gets a New Baby Giraffe"</i>. <p>The line read: <i>"Disney World's Animal Kingdom welcomed Willow, a one-year-old giraffe calf, on Tuesday, October 12, 2010!"</i></p>
